The Founder's Pitch Deck Guide

A seed deck wins on clarity, not volume: ten slides, three design rules, and one story an investor can repeat to their partners.

50+ YC decks analyzed $450M+ raised by those startups 10 slides that consistently appear <3 min avg. investor time per deck

How to use this guide

Investors usually decide whether to keep reading by slide 2. Start with the three facts and design rules below, then build each slide from the 10-slide structure, and run the pre-send checklist before your deck goes to anyone.

Why decks win or lose

Three facts shape everything else in this guide.

Speed of read

The first three slides decide it

If the point isn't obvious by slide 3, the rest rarely gets a fair read.

Memory over detail

Be the deck they can retell

Investors see hundreds of decks a year. The one they remember is the one they can retell in a sentence, not the one with the most data.

Design signals discipline

Clean slides read as clear thinking

A cluttered deck tells investors how the founder will run the company.

The three design rules

Every slide must pass all three before it goes in the deck. These come from YC partner Kevin Hale's guidance on seed decks.

Rule 1

Legibility

Large, bold fonts and high contrast. Readable from the back of a room or on a phone.

Test: Shrink the slide to a thumbnail. Can you still read the key line?
Rule 2

Simplicity

One idea per slide. No excess text, logos or diagrams cluttering the point.

Test: Name the slide's idea in five words. Need two ideas? Make two slides.
Rule 3

Obviousness

The takeaway lands in seconds without you narrating.

Test: Show it to a stranger for 5 seconds and ask what it said.

Practical defaults: 30pt+ body text, a headline that states the takeaway ("Revenue grew 3x in 6 months", not "Traction"), and one chart or image at most.

The 10-slide structure

This order answers investors' questions in the order they ask them. Select a slide to see what to include and what to avoid. If traction is your strongest point, move it up — the order serves the story.

Mistakes that kill decks before slide 5

Most decks are lost early to four avoidable mistakes.

MistakeFix
Overloading slides with information instead of one clean ideaOne headline and one supporting visual per slide; move detail to an appendix or data room
Illegible screenshots instead of simplified visualsCrop to the one part of the product that matters, or redraw it as a simple mock
Skipping the problem, solution or team slidesKeep all three — investors look for them and read their absence as a gap
Burying the ask, with no milestone attachedAmount and milestone on one slide: "$2M to reach $1M ARR in 18 months"

How to build your deck

Write the story before you open a slide tool. Start with slides and you end up decorating; start with the story and you end up persuading.

  1. 1Write your one-sentence pitchWhat you do, for whom, and why it matters now. If you can't write it, you aren't ready for slides.
  2. 2Draft the deck as ten headlinesOne takeaway sentence per slide. Read them aloud in order — they should tell the whole story with no visuals.
  3. 3Pick one proof per headlineA number, a chart, a quote or a product image. Only one.
  4. 4Build the slidesBig type, one idea, obvious takeaway. Everything else goes to an appendix.
  5. 5Run the stranger testShow each slide for 5 seconds to someone outside your industry. Rewrite any slide they can't summarize.
  6. 6Test the retellA day after a friend reads the deck, ask them to describe the company in two sentences. That is what an investor will tell their partners.
  7. 7Make two versionsA presenting version (sparse, you narrate) and a send-ahead version (fuller captions so it stands alone).
Remember: your goal isn't to impress investors with data. It's to make your story impossible to forget.
