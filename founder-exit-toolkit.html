NYC tax context

NYC founders pay federal, NY State and NYC city tax on exit proceeds — a combined blended rate of approximately 38.6%. This is meaningfully higher than neighboring states and makes the QSBS exclusion and post-exit trust planning especially valuable. A grantor trust — where the founder pays the trust's income tax personally — is particularly compelling at this rate, since it effectively becomes an additional tax-free gift to the trust each year.

NY estate tax — the cliff effect

New York's estate tax exemption is approximately $7.16M — but with a cliff effect: if an estate exceeds 105% of the exemption, the entire estate is taxed, not just the excess. On top of the federal estate tax of 40%, NYS can add up to a further 16% — a combined rate of up to 50%+ at death before anything reaches the next generation. This makes keeping assets outside the taxable estate — via a Dynasty Trust structured as an estate freeze — especially important for NYC-based founders. See Post Exit Tax Planning.
After an exit, the Dynasty Trust's proceeds can keep compounding under an Estate Freeze — a well-established, easily explained structure that uses a Grantor or Non-Grantor Trust election, with no specialized insurance product required. If the founder elects grantor trust status, they pay the trust's income tax personally each year — the trust grows tax-free, and the payment itself is treated as an additional tax-free gift to the trust. Combined with the assets sitting entirely outside the taxable estate, this can dramatically outperform holding everything personally. Federal and NY estate tax (40% federal above a $15M exemption; 16% NY above a $7.16M exemption, with a 105% cliff) are held fixed per current law; adjust the other illustrative assumptions below.
